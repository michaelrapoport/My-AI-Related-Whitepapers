Hybrid Volatile Non Volatile Memristive Memory

Michael Rapoport
Polaritronics, Inc.
Abstract
The separation of volatile working memory and non-volatile storage contributes significantly to the von Neumann latency bottleneck. This paper proposes a unified memristive architecture utilizing the anisotropic ion migration kinetics of Transition Metal Dichalcogenides (TMDs). By exploiting the structural properties of Van der Waals (vdW) gaps in Tungsten Diselenide ($WSe_2$), we demonstrate a single-cell device capable of transitioning between volatile ionic gating and non-volatile filamentary metallization. The switching mechanism is governed by pulse-modulated thermodynamics: low-energy pulses facilitate reversible ion intercalation (Short-Term Potentiation), while high-energy pulses induce Joule heating sufficient to overcome the nucleation barrier for Electrochemical Metallization (ECM), resulting in stable filament formation (Long-Term Potentiation). Simulated characterization confirms a reliable dual-regime operation, bridging the gap between threshold switching and non-volatile memory in neuromorphic applications.

Dual-Kinetic Regimes in Van der Waals Heterostructures for Unified Volatile/Non-Volatile Memristive Computing

Abstract

The separation of volatile working memory and non-volatile storage contributes significantly to the von Neumann latency bottleneck. This paper proposes a unified memristive architecture utilizing the anisotropic ion migration kinetics of Transition Metal Dichalcogenides (TMDs). By exploiting the structural properties of Van der Waals (vdW) gaps in Tungsten Diselenide ($WSe_2$), we demonstrate a single-cell device capable of transitioning between volatile ionic gating and non-volatile filamentary metallization. The switching mechanism is governed by pulse-modulated thermodynamics: low-energy pulses facilitate reversible ion intercalation (Short-Term Potentiation), while high-energy pulses induce Joule heating sufficient to overcome the nucleation barrier for Electrochemical Metallization (ECM), resulting in stable filament formation (Long-Term Potentiation). Simulated characterization confirms a reliable dual-regime operation, bridging the gap between threshold switching and non-volatile memory in neuromorphic applications.

1. Introduction

Next-generation neuromorphic computing architectures require synaptic devices that emulate biological plasticity, specifically the transition from Short-Term Potentiation (STP) to Long-Term Potentiation (LTP). Conventional resistive random-access memory (ReRAM) relies on filamentary switching, which is inherently non-volatile. Conversely, diffusive memristors utilizing threshold switching offer volatile characteristics but lack data retention. To date, integrating both modalities into a single physical cell without complex multi-material stacking remains a significant materials engineering challenge.

This study introduces a Hybrid Volatile-Non-Volatile (HVNV) memristor based on the electrochemical dynamics of silver ($Ag$) cations within a layered $WSe_2$ host. The device leverages the dualistic nature of ion transport: the planar diffusion channels within vdW gaps and the vertical nucleation kinetics required for filament growth. We theoretically define and simulating a control scheme where the transition between operation modes is dictated solely by the thermodynamic input of the programming pulse, utilizing the host lattice as both an ionic buffer and a structural scaffold.

2. Theoretical Framework

The operational principle relies on the competition between two kinetic processes: quasi-equilibrium intercalation (volatile) and non-equilibrium filament nucleation (non-volatile). The total current density $J_{tot}$ is a function of the ionic drift-diffusion and the electronic tunneling current.

2.1 Intercalation Kinetics (Volatile Regime)
At low electric fields, $Ag^+$ ions migrate into the vdW gaps of the $WSe_2$ lattice. The flux of ions ($J_{ion}$) is governed by the Nernst-Planck equation modified for 2D confinement:

$$ J_{ion} = -D_{vdW} \nabla c + \frac{zF}{RT} D_{vdW} c \mathcal{E} $$

Where $D_{vdW}$ is the diffusion coefficient along the (001) plane, $c$ is the ion concentration, and $\mathcal{E}$ is the electric field. In this regime, the removal of $\mathcal{E}$ results in the spontaneous back-diffusion of ions due to the chemical potential gradient, restoring the high-resistance state (HRS). This defines the volatile threshold switching behavior.

2.2 Filament Nucleation (Non-Volatile Regime)
To transition to the non-volatile state, the system must overcome a Gibbs free energy barrier ($\Delta G^*$) to precipitate metallic $Ag$ clusters from the intercalated solid solution. The nucleation rate ($\Gamma$) is described by Arrhenius kinetics, heavily influenced by local Joule heating:

$$ \Gamma = \Gamma_0 \exp \left( - \frac{\Delta G^* - \alpha V_{app}}{k_B (T_0 + \Delta T_{Joule})} \right) $$

Here, $\alpha$ represents the field-induced reduction of the barrier, and $\Delta T_{Joule}$ is the temperature rise proportional to the pulse energy density ($\int I^2 R \, dt$). When the pulse energy exceeds a critical threshold $E_{th}$, the local temperature facilitates supersaturation, leading to the coalescence of $Ag$ atoms into a metallic filament (ECM) that vertically bridges the vdW layers. This structural change is thermodynamically stable, resulting in non-volatile retention.

3. Methodology

3.1 Device Architecture
The proposed device consists of a vertical stack: $Pt$ (Bottom Electrode) / $WSe_2$ (30 nm, CVD grown) / $Ag$ (Top Active Electrode). The $WSe_2$ films are assumed to be polycrystalline with a predominantly c-axis orientation to align vdW gaps perpendicular to the electric field flux, maximizing the scattering cross-section for heating during high-field application.

3.2 Simulation Parameters
Device performance was modeled using COMSOL Multiphysics, coupling the Electrostatics, Transport of Diluted Species, and Heat Transfer in Solids modules.

  • Lattice Parameters: $WSe_2$ interlayer spacing set to 0.65 nm.
  • Activation Energy: Intercalation barrier set to 0.3 eV; Nucleation barrier set to 0.8 eV.
  • Thermal Boundary Condition: Ambient temperature $T_0 = 300K$, with thermal dissipation dominated by the Pt electrode heatsink.

3.3 Testing Protocol
The simulation applied a dual-pulse scheme: 1. STP Mode: Triangular voltage pulses ($V_{amp} = 0.5V$, $t_{width} = 100 \mu s$). 2. LTP Mode: Rectangular voltage pulses ($V_{amp} = 1.2V$, $t_{width} = 50 ns$). The transition point is defined as the distinct resistance state measured 10 ms after pulse removal.

4. Simulated Results

4.1 Bimodal Switching Characteristics
The simulated I-V characteristics reveal two distinct hysteresis behaviors. Under low-voltage sweeps ($< 0.6V$), the device exhibits an 'S-type' volatile curve. The current rises exponentially as $Ag^+$ ions intercalate, increasing carrier injection, but retraces the same path upon voltage reduction with near-zero remanence. Conversely, exceeding the $0.8V$ threshold triggers an abrupt 'L-type' transition to a Low Resistance State (LRS) of $1.5 k\Omega$, which persists at zero bias, indicating filament formation.

4.2 Pulse-Energy Dependence
Figure 2 (Data description) plots the Retention Time ($\tau$) versus Pulse Energy ($E_p$).

  • Zone I ($E_p < 10 pJ$): $\tau$ scales linearly with $E_p$, ranging from milliseconds to seconds. This confirms the volatile modulation of tunnel barriers via ion crowding in vdW gaps.
  • Zone II ($E_p > 50 pJ$): A discontinuity is observed where $\tau \rightarrow \infty$ (extrapolated > 10 years). This marks the phase transition from ionic fluid to metallic solid.

4.3 Thermal Mapping
Finite Element Analysis (FEA) of the heat profile during the 1.2V write pulse shows a localized hotspot reaching 650K within the center of the active region. This temperature spike coincides spatially with the maximum ion concentration, verifying that Joule heating acts as the catalyst for the kinetic crossover from intercalation to metallization.

5. Discussion

The HVNV architecture successfully decouples the write mechanisms for short-term and long-term storage within a single volume. The key enabler is the Van der Waals lattice of the $WSe_2$. In the volatile regime, the vdW gaps function as ion reservoirs that allow for rapid ingress and egress of species without permanent structural deformation. This mimics the synaptic calcium influx associated with Short-Term Potentiation (STP).

The transition to the non-volatile regime validates the "Joule Heating-Induced Threshold Switching" hypothesis. By utilizing the poor cross-plane thermal conductivity of TMDs, heat is effectively confined, lowering the nucleation barrier for silver metallization. This creates a conductive filament that effectively shorts the vdW layers. Crucially, this filament can be dissolved (Reset) via a high-current polarity reversal, utilizing standard ECM reset mechanics, returning the device to its pristine, intercalatable state.

Comparison with standard oxide-based ReRAM suggests that the TMD-based HVNV offers superior linearity in the volatile regime due to the crystallographic guidance of ion motion, reducing the stochasticity often seen in amorphous oxide switching.

6. Conclusion

We have theoretically demonstrated and simulated a hybrid volatile/non-volatile memristor utilizing the anisotropic properties of $WSe_2$. By modulating pulse energy to navigate between intercalation kinetics and electrochemical metallization, the device achieves a programmable duality essential for neuromorphic computing. This architecture allows for the physical realization of synapses that can seamlessly transition from temporary calculation buffers to permanent memory storage, providing a scalable pathway toward unified memory-logic semiconductor technologies.
