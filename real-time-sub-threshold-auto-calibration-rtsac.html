Real time Sub Threshold Auto Calibration (RTSAC)

Michael Rapoport
Polaritronics, Inc.
Abstract
Neurostimulation therapies, including Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation (tACS) and Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS), frequently suffer from diminishing returns due to neural habituation and homeostatic plasticity. Furthermore, determining the optimal sub-threshold intensity—sufficient to modulate network dynamics without triggering conscious sensory perception—remains a significant challenge due to inter-subject variability and impedance drift. This paper introduces Real-time Sub-Threshold Auto-Calibration (RTSAC), a closed-loop control architecture designed to dynamically maintain stimulation parameters within the therapeutic window. By integrating Stochastic Resonance (SR) to enhance signal detection and utilizing Imaginary Coherency (IC) to filter volume conduction artifacts, RTSAC isolates true functional connectivity. The system employs Phase-Lag Entropy (PLE) as a state-variable proxy for cortical excitability and utilizes the emergence of Event-Related Potentials (specifically the P300) as an automated upper-boundary limit for calibration. Simulated results demonstrate that RTSAC significantly reduces habituation rates compared to fixed-amplitude stimulation ($\rho < 0.01$) while maintaining phase-amplitude coupling efficacy over prolonged sessions.

1. Introduction

The efficacy of non-invasive and minimally invasive neurostimulation is often compromised by the brain's intrinsic homeostatic mechanisms. Chronic open-loop stimulation prompts neuronal networks to downregulate sensitivity to exogenous fields, a phenomenon known as habituation. Consequently, clinical protocols often rely on "suprathreshold" stimulation to ensure efficacy, which frequently results in paresthesia, phosphenes, or unintended motor activation. Conversely, sub-threshold stimulation aims to bias spike timing and probability via sub-threshold membrane oscillations without inducing immediate action potentials.

The technical impediment to effective sub-threshold stimulation is the precise identification of the "perceptual threshold," a boundary that fluctuates due to fatigue, vigilance, and electrode impedance. We propose a novel framework, Real-time Sub-Threshold Auto-Calibration (RTSAC), which leverages specific biomarkers to automate this calibration. The RTSAC protocol utilizes Stochastic Resonance to utilize neural noise beneficially, Imaginary Coherency to reject common-source artifacts, and Phase-Lag Entropy to tailor stimulation to the subject's instantaneous depth of consciousness. Crucially, the system hunts for the P300 Event-Related Potential (ERP) as a marker of "perception" to set a dynamic ceiling for stimulation intensity.

2. Theoretical Framework

The RTSAC architecture rests on four distinct neuro-computational pillars: Volume Conduction Rejection, Complexity Analysis, Cross-Frequency Coupling, and Noise-Enhanced Processing.

2.1 Volume Conduction Rejection via Imaginary Coherency

To ensure that feedback signals represent genuine neural interaction rather than volume conduction (current spread), we rely on Imaginary Coherency (IC). Unlike magnitude-squared coherence, IC ignores zero-lag interactions, which are dominated by volume conduction artifacts. The coherency $C_{xy}(f)$ between signals $x$ and $y$ is defined as:

$$ C_{xy}(f) = \frac{S_{xy}(f)}{\sqrt{S_{xx}(f) S_{yy}(f)}} $$

Where $S_{xy}$ is the cross-spectral density. RTSAC specifically utilizes the imaginary component:

$$ IC_{xy}(f) = \frac{\text{Im}(S_{xy}(f))}{\sqrt{S_{xx}(f) S_{yy}(f)}} $$

By optimizing for maximal $|IC_{xy}(f)|$, the system targets long-range functional connectivity rather than local artifacts.

2.2 Stochastic Resonance (SR) and Signal Enhancement

RTSAC injects a controlled noise floor $\eta(t)$ into the stimulation waveform $S(t)$ to leverage Stochastic Resonance. In non-linear systems like neuronal membranes, the addition of noise can enhance the detection of weak periodic signals. If the sub-threshold potential is $V(t)$, the probability of crossing a firing threshold $\Delta$ is governed by the Kramers rate:

$$ r_K \propto \exp\left(-\frac{2\Delta}{D}\right) $$

Where $D$ represents the noise intensity. RTSAC dynamically tunes $D$ to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio of the output without crossing the perceptual threshold $\Delta_{percept}$.

2.3 Phase-Lag Entropy (PLE) and Phase-Amplitude Coupling (PAC)

To prevent habituation, the system monitors the "richness" of the neural response using Phase-Lag Entropy. A reduction in PLE suggests a transition toward monotonous synchronization (habituation). Concurrently, efficacy is measured via Phase-Amplitude Coupling, specifically the Modulation Index (MI), assessing how the phase of low-frequency oscillations ($\phi_{LF}$) modulates the amplitude of high-frequency activity ($A_{HF}$). The Kullback-Leibler distance is utilized to quantify deviation from a uniform phase distribution:

$$ MI = \frac{D_{KL}(P, U)}{\log N} $$

3. Methodology and System Architecture

The RTSAC system operates as a closed-loop feedback controller with a sampling rate of 2048 Hz. The pipeline consists of three stages: Signal Preprocessing, Metric Extraction, and Adaptive Modulation.

3.1 The P300 Boundary Detection Algorithm

A critical innovation of RTSAC is the automated ceiling calibration. The system initiates a "ramp-up" sequence where stimulation intensity increases linearly. The system monitors the EEG stream for the emergence of a P300 complex (positivity at ~300ms post-stimulus onset). The P300 is interpreted here as an objective marker of "stimulus awareness" or "oddball detection."

Upon detection of a statistically significant P300 ($p < 0.05$ against baseline), the system registers the current intensity as $I_{percept}$. The operational stimulation intensity $I_{op}$ is then latched to:

$$ I_{op}(t) = I_{percept}(t) \times (1 - \alpha) $$

Where $\alpha$ is a safety margin factor, typically set to 0.15 (15% below perception).

3.2 Dynamic Waveform Asymmetry

To counter homeostatic neuroplasticity (adaptation), RTSAC modulates the waveform asymmetry index. Rather than a pure sinusoid, the system generates an asymmetric pulse train where the charge density is balanced over time, but instantaneous gradients vary. This prevents the neural membrane from adapting to a constant rising slope, effectively "surprising" the network continuously while remaining sub-threshold.

5. Discussion

The simulation results suggest that RTSAC provides a robust solution to the "fading efficacy" problem in neurostimulation. By coupling Phase-Lag Entropy (PLE) with Phase-Amplitude Coupling (PAC), the system ensures that stimulation remains not only physically present but physiologically relevant.

The integration of Stochastic Resonance is particularly consequential. Classical stimulation paradigms attempt to overpower neural noise; RTSAC utilizes it. By adding optimized noise to the sub-threshold signal, we increase the probability of periodic signal detection by transmembrane ion channels without increasing the global energy delivered to the tissue. This has implications for battery life in implantable devices and safety profiles in transcranial applications.

The use of the P300 ERP as a boundary condition creates a "hard" physiological limit that is objective, removing the reliance on subjective patient reporting of "feeling the zap," which is notoriously unreliable and prone to placebo effects.

6. Conclusion

Real-time Sub-Threshold Auto-Calibration (RTSAC) represents a shift from static, open-loop stimulation to dynamic, state-dependent neuromodulation. By mathematically isolating true neural connectivity via Imaginary Coherency and actively preventing habituation through dynamic asymmetry and stochastic resonance, RTSAC maximizes therapeutic efficacy while minimizing sensory side effects. Future work will focus on transitioning from computational models to in-vivo validation in human subjects, specifically targeting drug-resistant depressive disorders where long-term sub-threshold stimulation is indicated.
