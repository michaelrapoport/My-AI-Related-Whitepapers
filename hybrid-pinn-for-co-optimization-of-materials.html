Hybrid PINN for Co Optimization of Materials

Michael Rapoport
Polaritronics, Inc.
Abstract
The design and optimization of functional materials exhibiting coupled active (actuation) and passive (structural) responses present significant computational challenges. Traditional finite element methods (FEM) rely on phenomenological constitutive models that often lack flexibility, while purely data-driven machine learning approaches fail to adhere to fundamental conservation laws, leading to poor extrapolation. This paper introduces a novel Hybrid Physics-Informed Neural Network (Hy-PINN) architecture designed for the co-optimization of complex material systems. By embedding continuum mechanics principles directly into the network’s computational graph, specifically through the utilization of tensor invariants to ensure frame indifference and automatic differentiation to enforce thermodynamic consistency, we establish a robust modeling platform. The architecture employs a multiplicative gating mechanism to capture non-linear coupling between mechanical deformation and active stimuli (thermal/electric). Simulated results demonstrate that the Hy-PINN outperforms standard deep learning baselines in data efficiency and physical validity, providing a generalizable framework for accelerated material discovery.

A Hybrid Physics-Informed Neural Network Framework for the Constitutive Co-Optimization of Active-Passive Material Systems

Abstract

The design and optimization of functional materials exhibiting coupled active (actuation) and passive (structural) responses present significant computational challenges. Traditional finite element methods (FEM) rely on phenomenological constitutive models that often lack flexibility, while purely data-driven machine learning approaches fail to adhere to fundamental conservation laws, leading to poor extrapolation. This paper introduces a novel Hybrid Physics-Informed Neural Network (Hy-PINN) architecture designed for the co-optimization of complex material systems. By embedding continuum mechanics principles directly into the network’s computational graph, specifically through the utilization of tensor invariants to ensure frame indifference and automatic differentiation to enforce thermodynamic consistency, we establish a robust modeling platform. The architecture employs a multiplicative gating mechanism to capture non-linear coupling between mechanical deformation and active stimuli (thermal/electric). Simulated results demonstrate that the Hy-PINN outperforms standard deep learning baselines in data efficiency and physical validity, providing a generalizable framework for accelerated material discovery.

1. Introduction

Emerging classes of smart materials, such as dielectric elastomers, shape memory alloys, and magneto-active polymers, are defined by their ability to undergo large reversible deformations under external stimuli. The constitutive behavior of these materials is governed by a complex interplay between passive hyperelasticity and active multiphysics responses. The optimization of such materials requires an accurate mapping of the strain energy density function ($\Psi$), a scalar potential from which stress and other thermodynamic conjugates are derived.

Conventional constitutive modeling involves creating empirical mathematical structures and fitting parameters to experimental data. This process is often constrained by the bias of the chosen mathematical form. Conversely, deep neural networks (DNNs) offer universal approximation capabilities but suffer from a lack of interpretability and the violation of physical laws when predictions drift outside the training domain.

We propose a Hybrid PINN framework that bridges this gap. Unlike standard PINNs that solve boundary value problems for specific geometries, our approach focuses on constitutive discovery. We enforce the Principle of Material Frame Indifference (Objectivity) by rigorously preprocessing inputs into tensor principal invariants. Furthermore, we satisfy the Second Law of Thermodynamics (Clausius-Duhem inequality) by structuring the network to predict scalar potential energy, utilizing Automatic Differentiation (AD) to derive stress tensors, thereby guaranteeing path independence and energy conservation.

2. Theoretical Framework

2.1 Kinematics and Objectivity
Consider a continuum body undergoing finite deformation mapping $\boldsymbol{\chi}(\mathbf{X}, t)$. The deformation gradient is defined as $\mathbf{F} = \nabla_{\mathbf{X}} \boldsymbol{\chi}$. To ensure the constitutive model remains invariant under rigid body rotation (Frame Indifference), we rely on the Right Cauchy-Green deformation tensor, $\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{F}^T \mathbf{F}$.

A critical requirement for the neural network input is that it must map objective tensors to scalar energy. Therefore, rather than feeding the raw components of $\mathbf{C}$ into the network, we utilize its principal invariants ($I_1, I_2, I_3$), defined as:

$$ I_1 = \text{tr}(\mathbf{C}) $$ $$ I_2 = \frac{1}{2} [(\text{tr}(\mathbf{C}))^2 - \text{tr}(\mathbf{C}^2)] $$ $$ I_3 = \det(\mathbf{C}) $$

2.2 Thermodynamic Consistency and Automatic Differentiation
For a hyperelastic material subject to active stimuli (represented by a scalar field $\eta$, e.g., temperature or electric potential), the Helmholtz free energy density $\Psi$ acts as the potential. The Second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor ($\mathbf{S}$) is derived via the Coleman-Noll procedure:

$$ \mathbf{S} = 2 \frac{\partial \Psi(\mathbf{C}, \eta)}{\partial \mathbf{C}} $$

In standard machine learning approaches, a network might attempt to predict $\mathbf{S}$ directly from $\mathbf{C}$. This often leads to a non-conservative vector field where $\oint \mathbf{S} : d\mathbf{C} \neq 0$. In our framework, the neural network $\mathcal{N}_{\theta}$ approximates the potential $\Psi$. We then exploit the computational graph's Automatic Differentiation (AD) engine to compute the derivative with respect to the inputs:

$$ \mathbf{S}_{\text{pred}} = 2 \left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{N}_{\theta}}{\partial I_1} \frac{\partial I_1}{\partial \mathbf{C}} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{N}_{\theta}}{\partial I_2} \frac{\partial I_2}{\partial \mathbf{C}} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{N}_{\theta}}{\partial I_3} \frac{\partial I_3}{\partial \mathbf{C}} \right) $$

This structural constraint ensures that the predicted stress response is always thermodynamically consistent.

3. Methodology

3.1 Network Architecture
The proposed architecture, the Tensor-Gated Hybrid PINN (TG-HPINN), consists of two parallel processing branches that converge via a multiplicative interaction layer.

The Passive Branch accepts the mechanical invariants $[I_1, I_2, J=\sqrt{I_3}]$ and processes them through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with Softplus activation functions (to ensure differentiability and smoothness). The Active Branch accepts environmental state variables (temperature $T$, electric field vector $\mathbf{E}$) and transforms them into latent actuation features.

3.2 Multiplicative Gating for Co-Optimization
Materials with coupled responses often exhibit multiplicative interactions (e.g., thermal expansion scales the effective stiffness). Standard additive concatenation in neural networks fails to capture this efficiently. We introduce a gating operation $\odot$:

$$ \mathbf{h}_{\text{combined}} = \mathbf{h}_{\text{passive}} \odot \sigma(\mathbf{W}_g \mathbf{h}_{\text{active}} + \mathbf{b}_g) $$

where $\sigma$ is a sigmoid activation scaling the passive features based on the active state. The final output is the scalar energy density $\Psi$.

3.3 Physics-Informed Loss Function
The network is trained by minimizing a composite loss function $\mathcal{L}$ comprising data fidelity and physical regularization:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{stress}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{convexity}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}} $$

Here, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{stress}}$ is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the AD-derived stress and ground truth measurements. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{convexity}}$ penalizes violations of the Druker stability postulate (ensuring positive stiffness), and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ref}}$ ensures the energy is zero at the reference configuration ($\Psi(\mathbf{I}, 0) = 0$).

4. Simulated Results

4.1 Experimental Setup
To validate the invention, we generated synthetic data representing a compressible Neo-Hookean material augmented with a nonlinear thermal-softening term. The ground truth free energy was defined analytically. A training dataset was constructed using 500 loading paths in the principal strain space (uniaxial, biaxial, and shear), covering the temperature range $T \in [293K, 400K]$.

4.2 Performance Metrics
The TG-HPINN was compared against a standard Deep Neural Network (DNN) that maps strain directly to stress components.

  • Interpolation Accuracy: Both models achieved low error ($<1\%$) on the training set.
  • Thermodynamic Consistency: The standard DNN produced a "curl" in the stress field (energy generation loops) of magnitude $1.5 \times 10^{-2}$ J/m³, whereas the TG-HPINN maintained strict zero curl ($< 10^{-7}$ J/m³, attributed to numerical precision), validating the exact differential constraint.
  • Extrapolation: When tested on strain regimes 20% beyond the training data, the standard DNN error diverged exponentially. The TG-HPINN maintained a bounded error profile, as the underlying physics constraints guided the gradients.

4.3 Optimization of Material Parameters
Using the trained TG-HPINN as a surrogate model, we performed a gradient-ascent optimization to maximize the specific work output of the material per unit thermal input. The differentiability of the input space allowed for the identification of optimal invariant combinations, suggesting a microstructural design target with high $I_1$ sensitivity and low $I_2$ coupling.

5. Discussion

The results confirm that embedding tensor calculus and thermodynamic laws into the neural network architecture yields a model that is data-efficient and physically robust. The use of principal invariants $I_1, I_2, I_3$ successfully decoupled the material response from the coordinate frame, a crucial feature often overlooked in "black box" AI. Furthermore, the multiplicative gating mechanism proved essential in modeling the transition between the passive elastic regime and the thermally activated softening regime without requiring separate model branches.

By obtaining the stress via $\partial \Psi / \partial \mathbf{C}$, we ensure that the learnt constitutive laws are conservative. This allows the model to be seamlessly integrated into commercial Finite Element solvers (e.g., Abaqus UMAT) for macro-scale structural simulations, a capability lacking in non-conservative ML models.

6. Conclusion

This paper presented a Hybrid Physics-Informed Neural Network for the modeling and co-optimization of active-passive materials. By strictly adhering to the principles of continuum mechanics—specifically frame indifference and thermodynamic consistency—and leveraging the power of automatic differentiation, the proposed framework solves the identifiability and generalizability problems inherent in traditional data-driven mechanics. This invention represents a significant step forward in AI-accelerated materials discovery, enabling the rapid design of complex, multi-functional material systems with mathematical guarantees of physical validity.
