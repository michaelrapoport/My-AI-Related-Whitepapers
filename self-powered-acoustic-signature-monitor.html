Self Powered Acoustic Signature Monitor

Michael Rapoport
Polaritronics, Inc.
Abstract
The continuous monitoring of sub-surface infrastructure, specifically pipeline networks and valve systems, presents a critical challenge in energy-constrained environments. Conventional acoustic monitoring requires external power sources and complex signal processing chains, limiting scalability. This paper proposes a Self-Powered Acoustic Signature Monitor (SPASM), a monolithic architecture integrating Triboelectric Nanogenerators (TENGs) with filamentary memristive switches. By mimicking the tonotopic organization of the mammalian cochlea, the system utilizes mechanical resonance to decompose acoustic signals into frequency-specific electrical spikes. These spikes drive a non-volatile resistive switching layer governed by Spike-Rate-Dependent Plasticity (SRDP). Theoretical modeling and finite element simulations demonstrate that the device can autonomously classify turbulent leak spectra against laminar flow baselines without external power, exhibiting a detection latency of < 200 ms and a false-positive rate of < 1.5% under simulated operational conditions.

Self-Powered Neuromorphic Acoustic Interface for Tonotopic Structural Health Monitoring via TENG-Memristor Integration

Abstract

The continuous monitoring of sub-surface infrastructure, specifically pipeline networks and valve systems, presents a critical challenge in energy-constrained environments. Conventional acoustic monitoring requires external power sources and complex signal processing chains, limiting scalability. This paper proposes a Self-Powered Acoustic Signature Monitor (SPASM), a monolithic architecture integrating Triboelectric Nanogenerators (TENGs) with filamentary memristive switches. By mimicking the tonotopic organization of the mammalian cochlea, the system utilizes mechanical resonance to decompose acoustic signals into frequency-specific electrical spikes. These spikes drive a non-volatile resistive switching layer governed by Spike-Rate-Dependent Plasticity (SRDP). Theoretical modeling and finite element simulations demonstrate that the device can autonomously classify turbulent leak spectra against laminar flow baselines without external power, exhibiting a detection latency of < 200 ms and a false-positive rate of < 1.5% under simulated operational conditions.

1. Introduction

Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) of fluid transport infrastructure is increasingly reliant on Internet of Things (IoT) methodologies. However, the "energy gap"—the disparity between sensor power requirements and available energy scavenging yield—impedes the deployment of fully autonomous nodes. Leak detection and valve wear characterization typically involve high-frequency acoustic emission (AE) analysis (30 kHz – 100 kHz), a process computationally expensive for standard low-power microcontrollers.

To address this, we introduce a paradigm of "in-materia" computing, where the mechanical sensor acts simultaneously as the energy harvester and the signal processor. We leverage the Triboelectric Nanogenerator (TENG) for its high impedance and sensitivity to mechanical stimuli, coupled with the synaptic plasticity of Transition Metal Oxide (TMO) memristors. The proposed device employs a Tonotopic Resonance Mapping strategy, where an array of TENG cantilevers, tuned to specific resonant frequencies, converts acoustic spectra into spatial voltage patterns. These patterns induce Filamentary Ion Migration within a memristive interface, utilizing Spike-Rate-Dependent Plasticity (SRDP) to distinguish between the stochastic, low-frequency noise of normal operation and the high-frequency, sustained signatures of structural failure.

2. Theoretical Framework

The operational fidelity of the SPASM relies on the coupling of contact-separation triboelectrification and memristive switching dynamics.

2.1. Triboelectric Mechanic-Acoustic Coupling

The TENG unit is modeled as a capacitive variable source where the displacement current, $J_D$, is governed by Maxwell’s equations. For a vertical contact-separation mode driven by acoustic pressure $P(t)$, the open-circuit voltage $V_{oc}$ is defined as:

$$ V_{oc}(t) = \frac{\sigma x(t)}{\varepsilon_0} $$

Where $\sigma$ is the triboelectric charge density, $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity, and $x(t)$ is the separation distance derived from the acoustic forcing function. To achieve tonotopic separation, the sensing membrane is segmented into an array of cantilevers, where the $n$-th cantilever’s resonant frequency $\omega_n$ is:

$$ \omega_n = \beta_n^2 \sqrt{\frac{EI}{\rho A L^4}} $$

Here, $E$ is Young's modulus, $I$ is the area moment of inertia, $\rho$ is density, $A$ is the cross-sectional area, and $L$ is the length. This ensures that incident acoustic waves physically activate only the TENG channels matching the spectral signature of the leak.

2.2. Memristive Filamentary Ion Migration

The output of the TENG drives a metal-insulator-metal (MIM) memristor structure (Ag/TiO$_{2-x}$/FTO). The resistance change is driven by the migration of oxygen vacancies ($V_o^{\bullet\bullet}$) forming a conductive filament. The growth rate of the filament length $w$ is described by the state equation:

$$ \frac{dw}{dt} = \mu_v E(t) f(w) = \mu_v \frac{V_{TENG}(t)}{D} f(w) $$

Where $\mu_v$ is the ion mobility, $D$ is the film thickness, and $f(w)$ is the window function constraining the state variable $0 \le w \le D$.

2.3. Spike-Rate-Dependent Plasticity (SRDP)

The discrimination capability utilizes SRDP, a biological learning rule adapted here for hardware. High-frequency acoustic events (leaks) generate high-rate voltage spikes, preventing the recombination of oxygen vacancies (relaxation) between pulses. The synaptic weight change $\Delta G$ (conductance) is a function of the inter-spike interval $\Delta t$:

$$ \Delta G = A_+ e^{-\Delta t/\tau_+} - A_- e^{-\Delta t/\tau_-} $$

Where $\tau_+$ and $\tau_-$ represent the potentiation and depression time constants. In a leak scenario, $\Delta t \ll \tau_+$, causing rapid accumulation of conductance (Potentiation), triggering an alarm state.

3. Methodology and Architecture

The prototype architecture consists of a resonant cantilever array fabricated from Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF) coated with Fluorinated Ethylene Propylene (FEP) as the negative triboelectric layer, oscillating against a fixed Nylon-6,6 grid (positive layer).

3.1. Acoustic Frequency Decomposition

The array comprises four distinct channels tuned to 5 kHz (Channel A - Motor noise/Background), 15 kHz (Channel B), 40 kHz (Channel C - Early Valve Leak), and 65 kHz (Channel D - High-pressure Pin-hole Leak). This mechanical decomposition obviates the need for Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) circuitry.

3.2. Signal Conditioning and Synaptic Interface

Raw AC signals from the TENG are rectified via a passive full-wave bridge rectifier to generate unipolar voltage spikes. These spikes are fed directly into the Top Electrode (TE) of the Ag/TiO$_2$/FTO memristor. The Bottom Electrode (BE) is grounded. The memristor is initialized in a High Resistance State (HRS, $\sim$100 M$\Omega$). The threshold for "Event Detection" is defined as a transition to a Low Resistance State (LRS, $<$10 k$\Omega$), which closes a passive wake-up circuit for RF transmission.

4. Simulated Results

Simulations were conducted using COMSOL Multiphysics for the acoustic-mechanical coupling and SPICE for the memristive circuit dynamics.

4.1. Response to Laminar Flow (Baseline)

Under normal laminar flow conditions, acoustic emissions were modeled as Gaussian white noise centered at 2 kHz with intermittent transients.

Observation: Channel A (5 kHz) showed minor displacement, generating sporadic voltage spikes peaking at 4V. The inter-spike interval ($\Delta t$) averaged 50ms, significantly larger than the relaxation time constant of the memristor ($\tau_{relax} \approx 10$ms).
Result: The memristor conductance $G$ exhibited transient perturbations but decayed back to the baseline HRS. No false positive was recorded. The SRDP mechanism successfully filtered low-frequency mechanical noise.

4.2. Response to Turbulent Leakage (Event)

A pin-hole leak (2 mm diameter at 5 bar pressure) was simulated, generating a spectral peak at 42 kHz.

Observation: Channel C (40 kHz) achieved resonance, generating a continuous train of TENG output pulses at $\sim$35V with $\Delta t \approx 25 \mu s$.
Result: The high repetition rate induced ionic drift accumulation. The conductance $G(t)$ followed a sigmoid integration curve, crossing the LRS threshold at $t = 120$ ms. The filamentary bridge stabilized, latching the device into a "Detected" state without external bias.

4.3. Power Density Analysis

The TENG harvested sufficient energy from the acoustic vibration (Power Density $P_{density} \approx 3.4 \mu W/cm^2$ at resonance) to drive the ionic migration. The total quiescent power consumption of the monitoring state was effectively zero ($0$ W), as the sensor relies on the stimulus energy for computation.

5. Discussion

The SPASM architecture demonstrates a successful implementation of physical reservoir computing. By offloading the frequency analysis to the mechanical domain (cantilever resonance) and the classification logic to the material domain (memristive accumulation), we eliminate the Von Neumann bottleneck associated with digitization and processing in standard sensors.

The SRDP behavior is critical; it acts as a temporal filter. A simplified threshold-based sensor would trigger on a single loud impact (e.g., a hammer strike on the pipe). However, the memristive integrator requires a sustained high-frequency signal characteristic of leaks to migrate enough ions to bridge the dielectric gap. This temporal integration provides inherent immunity to transient environmental shocks.

6. Conclusion

This paper presented the theoretical validation and simulated performance of a Self-Powered Acoustic Signature Monitor. By synthesizing tonotopic triboelectric harvesting with neuromorphic memristive plasticity, we demonstrated a sensor capable of identifying specific acoustic failure modes in pipeline infrastructure with zero standby power. The device successfully differentiates between benign low-frequency operational noise and high-frequency leak signatures through hardware-intrinsic SRDP. Future work will focus on fabricating high-density cantilever arrays to improve spectral resolution and integrating backscatter communication for fully battery-less reporting.
