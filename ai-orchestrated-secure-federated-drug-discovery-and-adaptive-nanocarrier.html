Abstract
A novel platform integrating secure multi-party computation, federated machine learning with zero-knowledge proof validation, and AI-driven adaptive control of nanocarrier synthesis. The platform enables collaborative drug discovery while preserving data privacy and IP, reduces preclinical failure rates, and optimizes nanocarrier manufacturing through real-time AI-driven adjustments of microfluidic reactor parameters. This integrated approach accelerates drug development, reduces costs, and enhances the quality of nanocarrier-based drug delivery systems.

Distinction Over Prior Art

While federated learning and homomorphic encryption are known concepts, their application in a combined, verifiable, and decentralized drug discovery and nanocarrier synthesis platform is novel. Prior art in federated learning lacks mechanisms for verifiable data integrity, making it vulnerable to biased or malicious contributions. Similarly, implementations of homomorphic encryption often suffer from computational overhead and lack robust verification. This invention overcomes these limitations by integrating zero-knowledge proofs for auditable validation of model contributions and computational integrity within a decentralized network, a combination not readily apparent to a PHOSITA due to the complexity of integrating these technologies and the computational demands of biological data.

Claims

1. A system for secure multi-party drug discovery, comprising: a decentralized network; a plurality of computing devices associated with a plurality of pharmaceutical entities, each computing device configured to encrypt proprietary chemical datasets and AI algorithms using fully homomorphic encryption (FHE); the decentralized network configured to perform computations on the encrypted chemical datasets and AI algorithms using FHE; and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to verify the integrity and correctness of the computations and algorithms without exposing the underlying proprietary data or algorithms.
2. A federated machine learning system for collaborative drug discovery, comprising: a plurality of computing devices associated with a plurality of pharmaceutical entities, each computing device configured to train local AI models on proprietary drug discovery datasets; a secure multi-party computation (SMC) module configured to aggregate model updates from the plurality of computing devices into a global predictive model; and a zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) validation module configured to verify the integrity and validity of the model updates from each pharmaceutical entity before aggregation by the SMC module.
3. A distributed, AI-driven adaptive control system for nanocarrier synthesis, comprising: a network of microfluidic reactors, each reactor configured for continuous-flow nanocarrier synthesis; a plurality of in-situ spectroscopic sensors associated with each microfluidic reactor, the sensors configured to generate real-time data indicative of nanocarrier quality; and a federated AI/ML predictive control engine configured to receive the real-time data from the sensors and dynamically adjust reactor parameters to optimize nanocarrier quality and yield across the network.
4. The system of claim 1, wherein the computations performed on the encrypted chemical datasets include drug-target interaction predictions.
5. The system of claim 3, wherein the federated AI/ML predictive control engine exploits principles of non-equilibrium thermodynamics for guided self-assembly of nanocarriers.

Detailed Embodiments

The DeHoDDN embodiment consists of a decentralized network, such as a blockchain, where pharmaceutical companies operate nodes. Each node encrypts its chemical libraries and AI algorithms using Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE). When a drug-target interaction computation is initiated, the network performs the computation on the encrypted data. Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) are used to verify the integrity of the computation results and the algorithms used, ensuring no proprietary information is revealed. Data flows from each pharmaceutical entity to the decentralized network in encrypted form, computations are performed within the encrypted domain, and the results are verified using ZKPs before being released.

The ZKP-Validated Federated Drug Discovery embodiment involves multiple pharmaceutical companies training local AI models on their proprietary datasets. Each company's computing device trains a model and generates model updates. These updates are then passed through a Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) validation module, which cryptographically proves that the updates originate from valid, unique, and high-quality datasets. The validated updates are then securely aggregated into a superior global predictive model using Secure Multi-Party Computation (SMC). Data flows from each pharmaceutical entity to the SMC module, where model updates are aggregated. The ZKP module ensures the integrity of the data before aggregation.

The Nano-Orchestrator AI embodiment comprises a network of high-throughput microfluidic reactors. Each reactor is equipped with multi-modal in-situ spectroscopic sensors that feed real-time data into a federated AI/ML predictive control engine. This engine dynamically adjusts individual reactor parameters, such as flow rates and temperature, to maintain precise nanocarrier quality and optimize yield across the entire network. The AI/ML engine exploits principles of non-equilibrium thermodynamics for guided self-assembly. Data flows from the sensors to the AI/ML engine, which then sends control signals back to the microfluidic reactors to adjust their parameters.
